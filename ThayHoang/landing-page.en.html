Week two, plant number two

During the first week, watering is easy — every morning you step onto the balcony to check the new plant. Then work gets busy, and the schedule fades into vague memory.

The leaves start dropping. You cannot tell whether the plant is thirsty or waterlogged, so you water more, just to be safe.

The plant dies faster. And this is already your second one.

The real problem

A dead plant is not a laziness problem — it is a guessing problem

The label on the pot only gives you a species name. It does not tell you whether today is a watering day, or whether the plant is short of water or drowning in it. Every species has its own rhythm, but every pot arrives with the same kind of label.

  • Drooping leaves can mean thirst — or the first sign of root rot
  • Dry soil on top does not always mean a dry root ball underneath
  • By the time the mistake is obvious, the plant may already be past saving

The solution

One card, planted in the pot, speaking the language of a first-time grower

No gardening jargon. No moisture percentages. Just the watering rhythm for the species you actually own and plain-language signs you can compare with the plant in front of you.

  • 1

    A rhythm for each species

    There is no universal schedule — a succulent is not a pothos, and a pothos is not a peace lily.

  • 2

    Visible signs of thirst

    Specific descriptions of the leaf: curling, color change, crispness — not a number from a device.

  • 3

    The overwatering signs people miss

    New growers are often afraid of too little water. The card shows the other direction too, because overwatering is just as damaging.

How to use it

Three steps. None require a phone.

The card is designed to be read while your hand is holding the watering can, right on the balcony — not stored in a drawer after you buy the plant.

1

Plant the card near the pot edge

The 12 cm stem is built for outdoor light and rain, with lettering that stays readable through the season.

2

Look at the card before watering

Compare the rhythm on the card with your last watering — and know whether today is the day.

3

Look at the leaves, then compare

Are they curling or yellowing? The card tells you what the sign means and what to do next.

FAQ

Questions people ask before ordering

Are the cards sold separately or with a pot?+

Both. Buy a single card or a set for the plants you already own. Partner nurseries can also place a card in a new pot at checkout — no extra pot is required.

Can the card survive sun and rain?+

It is made from rigid, coated stock with a water-resistant finish. The stem is designed for splash, direct sun and regular outdoor watering without the lettering peeling away.

My balcony faces west and gets very hot. Does the card adjust?+

Yes. Each card includes a light-direction adjustment. When ordering, choose your balcony direction and the card will use the adjusted rhythm instead of an average number.

What if I no longer need to look at the card?+

That is the goal, not a failure. After a season, many growers only keep the card as a species label because they can read the plant themselves. Move it to the next pot.

What if my plant is not in the collection?+

Send us the plant name through the order page. We will reply within two business days and consider it for the next print run if enough growers request it.
