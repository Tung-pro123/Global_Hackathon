Why balcony plants keep dying in rented homes

Every dead plant is one more time someone believes they cannot grow plants.

It is not laziness. Every species needs a different care rhythm, but the label on the pot only gives a name. A name does not tell anyone whether today is a watering day — so the grower has to guess.

A familiar situation

Nobody becomes a bad plant parent on purpose.

WEEK 01
Bring the plant home, place it on the balcony. Water regularly and check on it every day.
WEEK 02
Work gets busy. The watering schedule fades into vague memory.
THE SIGN
Leaves drop. Underwatered or overwatered? No ordinary label can answer.
THE REFLEX
Water more, just in case. Root rot sets in, and the plant dies faster.

An illustration of a recurring mistake — not measured data from one specific grower.

BALCONY CORNER
03
The key insight

The problem is not forgetting to water.
The problem is not knowing how to read the plant.

A reminder still makes people guess. A visible sign does not.

BALCONY CORNER
04
The solution

One card,
right at the roots.

A hard, moisture-resistant card planted directly in the soil. One card per species, with its watering rhythm and visible signs of too little or too much water — written for someone who has never grown a plant, not for a nursery expert.

No power required
No batteries required
Pothos
Twice a week; water when soil is dry
Yellow lower leaves = too much water
Drooping leaves = too little water
